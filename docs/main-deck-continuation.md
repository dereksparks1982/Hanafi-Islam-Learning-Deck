# Main Deck Continuation — Everyday Islamic Speech

## Purpose

The Main Deck currently ends at Card 146. The next main-deck sequence should teach the short Arabic expressions Muslims use throughout ordinary life, not merely define them.

The goal is practical recognition and correct use:

**What does it mean? When should I say it? What should I not use it for? What does another person reply, if anything?**

This sequence remains part of the Main Deck and continues the existing numbering rather than becoming a separate expansion.

## Design principle

Every card in this sequence should show:

- the expression in Arabic;
- a readable Latin transliteration;
- a concise English meaning;
- **WHEN TO SAY IT**;
- **EXAMPLES** from normal life;
- **DO NOT CONFUSE IT WITH** when needed;
- an appropriate Qur'an, hadith, or scholarly source line;
- any reply another Muslim should make when the Sunnah establishes one.

Repeated exposure to the Arabic is intentional. The learner should gradually recognize expressions visually as well as understand them.

## Proposed Cards 147–170

### Card 147 — EVERYDAY ISLAMIC EXPRESSIONS
Guide card explaining that these phrases are remembrance, praise, supplication, greetings, or ordinary Arabic depending on the expression. Not every Arabic phrase is a religious formula.

### Card 148 — BISMILLAH
**Arabic:** بِسْمِ اللَّهِ  
**Meaning:** In the name of Allah.

Teach using it when beginning a lawful act while seeking Allah's blessing and help, with specific Sunnah examples such as eating. Cross-reference existing cards where a longer prescribed duʿā already exists.

### Card 149 — ALHAMDULILLAH
**Arabic:** الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ  
**Meaning:** All praise belongs to Allah.

Use for praising and thanking Allah: after receiving a blessing or good news, after eating, when answering how one is doing, and after sneezing when one is the person who sneezed.

### Card 150 — IN SHĀʾ ALLĀH
**Arabic:** إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ  
**Meaning:** If Allah wills.

Use when speaking sincerely about something intended or hoped for in the future. Primary Qur'anic anchor: Qur'an 18:23–24.

### Card 151 — MĀ SHĀʾ ALLĀH
**Arabic:** مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ  
**Meaning:** What Allah has willed.

Use when seeing or speaking about something admirable, beautiful, or pleasing while recognizing that it exists by Allah's will. Qur'anic anchor: Qur'an 18:39. The card should distinguish this from merely using the phrase as a superstition or charm.

### Card 152 — SUBḤĀNALLĀH
**Arabic:** سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ  
**Meaning:** Glory be to Allah / Allah is free of imperfection.

Teach its use as tasbīḥ and in moments that prompt wonder at Allah's creation or recognition of His perfection.

### Card 153 — ALLĀHU AKBAR
**Arabic:** اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ  
**Meaning:** Allah is greater / Allah is the Greatest.

Teach takbīr in worship and ordinary remembrance, while cross-referencing the salah cards where it has a prescribed ritual role.

### Card 154 — ASTAGHFIRULLĀH
**Arabic:** أَسْتَغْفِرُ اللَّهَ  
**Meaning:** I seek Allah's forgiveness.

Use after sin, error, careless speech, or whenever seeking forgiveness and returning to Allah.

### Card 155 — LĀ ILĀHA ILLĀ ALLĀH
**Arabic:** لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ  
**Meaning:** There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah.

Teach it as the central declaration of tawḥīd and a form of dhikr, with a cross-reference to the Shahada card.

### Card 156 — LĀ ḤAWLA WA LĀ QUWWATA ILLĀ BILLĀH
**Arabic:** لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ  
**Meaning:** There is no power and no strength except through Allah.

Teach it as remembrance expressing dependence upon Allah, including the authentic description of it as a treasure from Paradise.

### Card 157 — ḤASBUNALLĀHU WA NIʿMA AL-WAKĪL
**Arabic:** حَسْبُنَا اللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ الْوَكِيلُ  
**Meaning:** Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best Disposer of affairs.

Use in fear, pressure, threat, or reliance upon Allah. Qur'anic anchor: Qur'an 3:173.

### Card 158 — INNĀ LILLĀHI WA INNĀ ILAYHI RĀJIʿŪN
**Arabic:** إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ  
**Meaning:** Indeed we belong to Allah, and to Him we return.

Use when calamity or loss strikes. Qur'anic anchor: Qur'an 2:156. Cross-reference Card 127.

### Card 159 — JAZĀKALLĀHU KHAYRAN
**Arabic:** جَزَاكَ اللَّهُ خَيْرًا  
**Meaning:** May Allah reward you with good.

Use to thank someone with a duʿā for reward rather than treating it merely as an Arabic substitute for “thanks.” Include the common feminine/plural forms where useful.

### Card 160 — BĀRAKALLĀHU FĪK
**Arabic:** بَارَكَ اللَّهُ فِيكَ  
**Meaning:** May Allah bless you.

Use as a duʿā asking Allah to place blessing in a person. Include feminine/plural forms if space permits.

### Card 161 — AS-SALĀMU ʿALAYKUM
**Arabic:** السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ  
**Reply:** وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلَامُ

Teach initiating and returning the Muslim greeting, including the obligation to return a greeting appropriately. Qur'anic anchor: Qur'an 4:86.

### Card 162 — WHEN SOMEONE SNEEZES
Teach the Sunnah exchange:

1. the sneezer says **Alḥamdulillāh**;
2. the listener says **Yarḥamukallāh**;
3. the sneezer replies **Yahdīkumullāhu wa yuṣliḥu bālakum**.

This card should make clear who says which phrase.

### Card 163 — ĀMĪN
**Arabic:** آمِين  
**Meaning:** O Allah, answer / accept.

Teach when it is said after supplication and its place after al-Fātiḥah in prayer, with Hanafi-specific wording checked before publication.

### Card 164 — ﷺ: ṢALLALLĀHU ʿALAYHI WA SALLAM
**Arabic:** صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلَّمَ  
**Meaning:** May Allah's blessings and peace be upon him.

Teach saying ṣalawāt when Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is mentioned. Qur'anic anchor: Qur'an 33:56.

### Card 165 — رضي الله عنه / عنها / عنهم
**Transliteration:** Raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu / ʿanhā / ʿanhum  
**Meaning:** May Allah be pleased with him / her / them.

Teach the honorific commonly used for the Companions, including the gender/plural forms.

### Card 166 — RAḤIMAHULLĀH
**Arabic:** رَحِمَهُ اللَّهُ  
**Meaning:** May Allah have mercy on him.

Teach the duʿā commonly said for deceased Muslim scholars and other deceased Muslims, with feminine form where useful.

### Card 167 — YALLA IS NOT YĀ ALLĀH
**Yalla — يلا:** colloquial Arabic meaning roughly “come on,” “let's go,” or “hurry.” It is ordinary speech, not a dhikr formula.  
**Yā Allāh — يَا اللَّهُ:** “O Allah,” directly calling upon Allah in duʿā.

This distinction is especially useful for an Arabic learner because the two can sound similar to a beginner while meaning completely different things.

### Card 168 — ALLĀHU AʿLAM
**Arabic:** اللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ  
**Meaning:** Allah knows best.

Teach using it with humility when one does not know something with certainty, especially in religious discussion, rather than pretending to knowledge one does not possess.

### Card 169 — WALLĀHI: OATHS ARE SERIOUS
**Arabic:** وَاللَّهِ  
**Meaning:** By Allah.

Explain that swearing by Allah is an oath, not conversational decoration. It should not be used casually or falsely. Include **billāhi** and **tallāhi** as related oath forms and source the Hanafi legal notes carefully. Qur'anic anchor for oaths: Qur'an 5:89.

### Card 170 — AʿŪDHU BILLĀH
**Arabic:** أَعُوذُ بِاللَّهِ  
**Meaning:** I seek refuge in Allah.

Teach when seeking Allah's protection, and distinguish the general phrase from the fuller **Aʿūdhu billāhi min ash-shayṭānir-rajīm** used before Qur'anic recitation and in other established contexts. Cross-reference existing Cards 33 and 139.

## The five phrases that triggered this sequence

The minimum learner outcome is that a person can answer these questions without guessing:

- **Bismillah:** before beginning a lawful action, especially where the Sunnah specifically teaches it.
- **Alhamdulillah:** when praising or thanking Allah for blessings, and in prescribed contexts such as after sneezing or eating.
- **In shāʾ Allāh:** about future intentions or hopes, acknowledging that they occur only if Allah wills.
- **Mā shāʾ Allāh:** when admiring or acknowledging something Allah has willed and bestowed.
- **Yalla:** ordinary colloquial Arabic for “let's go / come on,” not a devotional phrase. Do not confuse it with **Yā Allāh**, calling upon Allah.

## Review rule

These are **concept cards, not yet published cards**. Before they enter the Main Deck:

1. Arabic and transliteration must be checked;
2. Qur'an and hadith sourcing must be named precisely;
3. any Hanafi legal claim must be checked against a named Hanafi source;
4. duplicates with existing Main Deck cards should be cross-referenced rather than needlessly repeating entire cards;
5. the maintainer reviews the exact wording and layout before publication;
6. qualified imam/scholar feedback can then be incorporated under the normal correction workflow.
