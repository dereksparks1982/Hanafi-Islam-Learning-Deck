# Hanafi Learning Deck Qur'an Transliteration Standard

## Purpose

This transliteration is a **learning and pronunciation aid** for readers who are moving toward reading the Qur'an directly in Arabic. It is not a substitute for Arabic script, tajwid study, or learning from a qualified teacher.

The system is designed around the Hafs 'an 'Asim reading used by the project. It aims to represent the way a passage is actually read more closely than a simple letter-for-letter Romanization would.

Ordinary consonants use their familiar Latin equivalents where possible.

## Long vowels

Long vowels are marked consistently:

- **ā** for a long *a*
- **ī** for a long *i*
- **ū** for a long *u*

The macron is meaningful. It tells the learner that the vowel is held longer than a short vowel.

## Shaddah

A consonant carrying **shaddah** is doubled in transliteration when doing so helps the learner hear the actual pronunciation.

Examples:

- إِيَّاكَ → **iyyāka**
- ٱلضَّآلِّينَ → **ḍ-ḍāllīn** within connected pronunciation

## The definite article and sun-letter assimilation

The Arabic definite article **al-** changes in pronunciation before a sun letter. The project transliteration normally represents the **pronounced form**, not merely the underlying spelling.

Examples:

- ٱلرَّحْمَٰن → **ar-Raḥmān**
- ٱلصِّرَاط → **aṣ-Ṣirāṭ** when read as an independent phrase

Inside connected Qur'anic recitation, the transliteration may show the liaison more directly, for example **r-Raḥmān** or **ṣ-ṣirāṭ**, when the preceding vowel flows into the article.

## Hamzat al-wasl

**Hamzat al-wasl** is pronounced when beginning from the word but normally drops in connected recitation. Because this project is intended to teach actual reading, connected transliteration may reflect that elision.

Example:

- ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَاطَ → **ihdinā ṣ-ṣirāṭa**

A later word-by-word learning view may show the independent starting form separately where that is educationally useful.

## 'Ayn and hamzah

The project distinguishes:

- **ʿ** = ع (*ʿayn*)
- **ʾ** = ء (*hamzah*)

Hamzah may be left visually implicit where ordinary English spelling already makes the onset clear and adding the mark would make a beginner's line harder to read. 'Ayn should remain marked because omitting it can obscure the Arabic consonant entirely.

## Ta marbutah

**ة** may be represented according to how it is pronounced in context:

- usually **-h / -ah** when stopping on the word;
- **-t-** when grammatically connected and actually pronounced that way.

## Alif maqsura

**ى** representing a long final *a* is transliterated **ā**.

## Connected reading and stopping

The default ayah transliteration represents **connected reading within the ayah and a stop at the ayah boundary**. This is important because Qur'anic pronunciation can differ between continuation and stopping.

The project should not silently change a transliteration merely to make it look more familiar in English. A correction should be tied to the Arabic text, Hafs pronunciation, or a documented review decision.

## Capitalization and punctuation

Capitalization is used for readability rather than to imply a feature of Arabic grammar. English punctuation may be added sparingly to help a learner follow a long ayah, but it must not change the Arabic text or imply that punctuation is part of the revealed wording.

## Review rule

Every published page should be reviewed **ayah by ayah** for:

1. agreement with the verified Arabic source;
2. correct consonants and long vowels;
3. shaddah;
4. hamzat al-wasl and connected pronunciation;
5. sun-letter assimilation;
6. stopping at the ayah boundary; and
7. consistency with previously approved pages.

When there is uncertainty, the transliteration stays marked **under review** until the pronunciation is checked against a reliable recitation/reference or a qualified Arabic/Qur'an teacher.

## Page 1 working forms

The Page 1 prototype uses these working transliterations:

1. **Bismi llāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm**
2. **Al-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn**
3. **Ar-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm**
4. **Māliki yawmi d-dīn**
5. **Iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn**
6. **Ihdinā ṣ-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm**
7. **Ṣirāṭa lladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim ghayri l-maghḍūbi ʿalayhim wa-lā ḍ-ḍāllīn**

These are project-created working forms for the prototype and remain subject to page-by-page review before the Qur'an Reader is treated as a completed edition.
