Legal and source policy

Purpose and ownership

The Hanafi Learning Deck is a noncommercial Islamic learning project. It is not built around advertising, subscriptions, paywalls, behavioral tracking, or the commercial sale of access to religious material.

The project does not claim ownership of the Qur'an itself. The Arabic Qur'an is treated as revelation from Allah ﷻ and as sacred source text that must not be silently altered.

Human work surrounding the Qur'an is tracked separately. This includes translations, transliterations, annotations, typography, software, databases, page metadata, recordings, photographs, and other editorial or technical work. Sources and translators should be identified as clearly as possible.

Shariah discussion

Intellectual-property disagreement

Contemporary Muslim scholars have disagreed over whether modern intellectual-property rights are recognized by Shariah. The project records that disagreement instead of pretending there is one unanimous position.

Mufti Muhammad Taqi Usmani records a contemporary scholarly position rejecting intellectual property as a form of private ownership, including the reasoning that classical ownership concerned tangible property and that knowledge should not simply be monopolized. He also records the opposing position and ultimately accepts intellectual-property rights.

Source: Copyright According to Shariah

The International Islamic Fiqh Academy adopted the other position in Resolution No. 43 (5/5), recognizing literary production, copyrights, and patent rights as rights protected by Shariah.

Source: International Islamic Fiqh Academy Resolution No. 43 (5/5)

This page records the project's religious and scholarly position. It does not claim that civil copyright law ceases to exist, and it does not claim that every third-party work used, hosted, embedded, or linked by the project is covered by the project's own license.

Access to Islamic knowledge

Project operating position

  • Keep access to core Islamic learning material free of charge.
  • Carry no advertising or commercial paywall around the Qur'an Reader or core religious study material.
  • Identify translators and source editions rather than presenting externally produced work anonymously.
  • Preserve source provenance and version information where practical.
  • Distinguish the Arabic Qur'an from human translation, commentary, film, dramatization, and other secondary material.
  • Create original transliteration and explanatory material where practical.
  • Prefer public-domain, permissively licensed, or openly redistributable sources when they meet the required scholarly standard.
  • Document restrictive terms when the strongest available scholarly source carries them rather than pretending those terms do not exist.
  • Review source or rights disputes on their merits and decide whether material should remain, be corrected, be replaced, be independently recreated, or be removed.

Third-party material

What the project does and does not claim

The repository's CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license applies to original Hanafi Learning Deck material only to the extent that the project has the right to license it.

Third-party material does not automatically become project property merely because it is present in the repository, displayed in the Web App, hosted by the project, embedded from another service, or linked from project documentation. Externally created translations, photographs, films, recordings, historical documents, datasets, and software libraries remain distinct from project-created interface and integration work.

The Media section treats films as historical or educational material, not as Qur'an, hadith, fiqh, or a substitute for properly sourced study. Provenance should be retained where known. Unknown provenance is left unknown rather than invented.

Privacy

No behavioral tracking by the Hanafi Learning Deck

The project does not intentionally run advertising networks, behavioral analytics, tracking pixels, or social-login requirements. The goal is to minimize unnecessary third-party contact and keep ordinary use of the Web App as direct as practical.

External websites reached through deliberate outbound links have their own privacy practices. The project does not control those sites. The Web App should avoid loading third-party resources automatically when a local or self-hosted alternative is practical.

Rights and source claims

What to include in a complaint or request

To make a source, licensing, attribution, or rights complaint useful, please identify as specifically as possible:

  • the exact file, page, passage, image, recording, film, dataset, or other material at issue;
  • the person or entity asserting the claim;
  • the source, edition, or work involved;
  • the right, restriction, license term, attribution issue, or other basis being asserted;
  • supporting documentation or an authoritative reference where available; and
  • the action or remedy being requested.

The project can then examine the issue against its source record, attribution history, applicable terms, relevant civil-law questions where necessary, and the documented Shariah analysis.
